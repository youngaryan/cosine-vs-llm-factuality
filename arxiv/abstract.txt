Embedding cosine similarity is widely used to check whether generated answers are supported by evidence or match a reference, but it is rarely tested on the errors that matter: a single changed fact. We compare lexical overlap, two embedding models, BERTScore, a zero-shot NLI scorer, a feature-based risk model and two locally served LLM judges on RAGTruth, FaithBench, EVOUNA-NQ and HaluEval-QA, using thresholds fitted only on held-out calibration data. We add a counterfactual test with 6,145 single-fact flips in eight error types, each placed in the same sentence as a meaning-preserving paraphrase. A flip lowers cosine in 86.1% of pairs, yet with natural-data thresholds cosine changes its decision in only 12.0%, and it scores the error at least as high as the paraphrase in 36.6% of cases (NLI: 12.8%). On natural data cosine ranks no better than token overlap and adds nothing to the risk model. No evaluator's 5% false-acceptance threshold survived a change of domain. A risk-gated cascade beat judging everything with a 3B judge that was unreliable on long evidence. With a 14B judge, gating saved judge calls at an accuracy cost our pilot subset could not resolve. All costs are measured on one CPU laptop. Cosine is a weak feature, not a factuality check. Code and results: https://github.com/youngaryan/cosine-vs-llm-factuality.
