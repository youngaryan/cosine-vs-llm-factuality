# Internal peer review (17 November 2026)

Written after the final analysis (2,000 cluster-bootstrap resamples). It answers the twelve quality-control
questions of the study brief and lists the manuscript changes they caused. Numbers come from `results/*.json`
through `paper/generated/numbers.tex`.

| # | Question | Answer | Evidence / action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the contribution materially different from published work? | **Partly.** Comparing cosine with NLI and judges, threshold-transfer failure and selective judging all have precedents (see `NOVELTY_AUDIT.md` §1). What is new is the combination on human-labelled long RAG answers, and the matched-locus inversion rate. | Intro contributions are framed as an evaluation study, not a new method; the cascade is described as a standard two-threshold scheme. |
| 2 | Is the novelty claim supported by the literature review? | Yes, within the 41 verified references. A newer or unindexed paper could have done the same, so the claim is worded "to our knowledge". | `NOVELTY_AUDIT.md` §3–4. |
| 3 | Are factual correctness and evidence faithfulness defined consistently? | Yes. Faithfulness (RAGTruth, FaithBench, HaluEval-QA, counterfactuals) and reference correctness (EVOUNA-NQ) are scored, thresholded and reported separately and never pooled. | Method; separate main and EVOUNA tables. |
| 4 | Is the ground truth independent of the evaluated LLM judges? | Yes. Labels come from the human annotations of the datasets (synthetic for HaluEval-QA) or from the rule that created a counterfactual. Judge output is never a label. | Setup; Limitations. |
| 5 | Are the baselines strong enough? | Adequate for a cheap-evaluator study, not state of the art. No trained grounding detector (MiniCheck, AlignScore) and no frontier API judge was run. The third embedder was dropped (Deviation 1). | Stated in Limitations and Discussion as the main gap. |
| 6 | Are all model comparisons fair? | Yes. Every judge comparison uses cheap methods on exactly the same examples, and all thresholds come from the same calibration split. The 3B and 14B judges are compared on their nested overlap only. | Judged-subset, counterfactual-judge and cascade tables. |
| 7 | Are synthetic labels validated and described appropriately? | They are rule-validated, **not** human-validated. The 160-pair audit sheet is unreviewed, and the paper says so wherever counterfactual numbers appear. The paraphrase filter is automatic. | Method; Appendix deviations; Limitations. |
| 8 | Are train/calibration/test boundaries free of leakage? | Yes, as checked. Splits are by source group. The risk model is trained on the RAGTruth training split and thresholds are fitted on the calibration split. Counterfactual bases are drawn only from test-split faithful answers (verified: all 1,517 bases are `split == test`). The perturbation rules were debugged on training-split examples. | `tests/test_*.py`: group-leakage and test-only-base checks. |
| 9 | Are statistical differences meaningful? | The confirmatory results are: H1 versus NLI (+6.4 points, Holm p ≤ .002); H1 versus the small judge **not supported** (Holm p = .615); H2 (all p ≤ .0005); H3 (7 of 20 cells, Holm p = .02); H4 coverage (+9.0 points, Holm p ≤ .002, with the accuracy condition met for the small judge). The 14B-judge analyses are exploratory; with n = 36 per task they are reported as qualitative. Several bootstrap p-values sit at the 1/2000 floor and are reported as bounds. | Results text revised to give intervals and say "not significant" where intervals include zero. |
| 10 | Are performance and cost claims backed by real measurements? | Yes. All costs come from one clean single-process benchmark (`results/cost.json`, 90 answers, 30 calls per judge row). No dollar figures are given, because no API was used. Bulk timing files are not used for claims. A claim about GPU ratios was removed because it was not measured. | Cost subsection; cost table; Pareto figure. |
| 11 | Do results hold on datasets not used during calibration? | Mostly **no**, and that is a reported finding. Thresholds broke the 5% budget off-domain for every evaluator, and the risk model's calibration error rose from .117 to above .4 on FaithBench and HaluEval-QA. On HaluEval-QA, high AUCs reflect a length artefact. | Threshold-transfer subsection; Discussion. |
| 12 | Can another researcher reproduce the findings? | Yes, given the same datasets and Ollama model tags. Seeds, configurations, prompts, raw judge outputs and all scripts are included, and every table and number is regenerated by script. Reproducing all the judge results takes about a working day on a CPU. Small differences from Ollama/llama.cpp builds are possible; recorded verdicts allow re-analysis without re-running the judges. | `README.md`; `python src/run_all.py`. |

## Claims revised during this review

* "No diagonal cosine cell exceeds 5%" was false after the final run (the FaithBench diagonal is 10.8%, lower
  bound 5.4%). The Results, Introduction and Discussion now say that most evaluators exceed the budget in one or
  two of their own domains, and that only the full risk model stays within it in every domain.
* "The 14B judge beats every cheap method on RAGTruth" was narrowed to the pooled 108-answer subset, because
  on RT-QA BERTScore and the cosine-plus-checks risk model score higher than it.
* "Better than NLI on RT-D2T" was removed, because that interval includes zero.
* "Addition is the error type that dominates RAGTruth" became "the most common annotated type".
* "NLI detects baseless information *slightly* more often" lost "slightly" (75.1% vs 65.9%).
* An unmeasured statement about GPU cost ratios was deleted.
* The Pareto figure no longer shows 14B-subset points on the 3B-subset axes; it now has one panel per subset.

## Residual weaknesses a reviewer would raise

1. The judge sample is small (36 answers per RAGTruth task for the 14B judge), and only two open models were used.
2. There is no trained grounding detector (MiniCheck, AlignScore) and no frontier judge.
3. Counterfactual labels have no human audit.
4. All costs come from one CPU laptop.
5. The PDF is compiled with Tectonic and was checked page by page. That check led to widening several tables,
   fixing the `--` flags in the reproduction listing, and correcting one wrong number (the length-only AUC on
   HaluEval-QA had been taken from the RAGTruth column). A few overfull lines under 11 pt remain.
